Methodist High School Showcase Teaching Guide

2026-10-19 · Olusoji Daniel Ayoola

The approach: teach Real vs Fake deeply, then transfer

Teach Real vs Fake in depth (60 minutes at most) with all three teams watching. Then the Honest Scale and Family Job Board teams find the same parts in their own game in 40 minutes, led by their Planner, while you check. Do not teach all three in full: they share one skeleton, and a full second and third walk-through costs about two more hours of small children's attention.

Why Real vs Fake is the anchor. Its whole round is decided by one IF: "Is the side you picked the side the fake is on?" Small children grasp that in one go, and it is the same shape as the big IF in the other two games ("Is the needle equal to what they wanted?", "Is Mum calling for MY chore?"). It also shows a random pick, a list, a timer and a loop within one 2-minute round.

The skeleton all three games share:

  1. Green flag: every sprite hides; the Stage runs set up the rules.

  2. The Stage shouts show title; the title, buttons and characters appear.

  3. PLAY shouts start game; a repeat until loop runs the rounds (products, customers or chores).

  4. Inside each round: a timer counts, the player acts, one IF judges.

  5. The boxes (score, hearts, trust, streak) change.

  6. The ending IFs pick the result and the game star.

Sharing responsibilities

At every moment three jobs are running: one student drives (hands on the mouse), the Planner runs the clock and the checklist, and every other speaker listens for their own word and writes one line on the back of their speech card. Nobody is ever just watching.

The Planner is always active. Pick the most organised child, not the best coder. The Planner:

  1. Holds a phone timer and calls "Switch!" when a scene's time is up.

  2. Reads the next step aloud and names the next driver.

  3. Ticks the checklist and gives the thumbs-up when someone finds their block.

  4. Keeps the Trouble Log: one line each time something broke and how it was fixed (this becomes "the roadblocks we met").

  5. Saves each new version of the game with a new name and collects everyone's note lines at the end.

The Planner can also hold the Opener or Closer card. In a 5-student team some children hold two cards and listen for both words.

The driver is whoever the scene belongs to. Scene about boxes: the Box Keeper drives. The hand that did it is the mouth that explains it to the judges.

Three class rules:

  • Thumbs up when you hear your word. You see at a glance who is lost.

  • One line, own words, pencil. If a child can write it in their own words, they can say it to a judge.

  • New scene, new driver. Nobody keeps the mouse two scenes in a row.

What each game does, start to finish

Every child must be able to tell their game's process as a chain: what the player does, what the game does with it, and what it finally shows. Each step below names the speaker who owns it, so the chain is shared out and the team tells it together. The Closer copies their game's chain onto one card.

The 30-second pattern every child practises: "First you ___. The game remembers ___. It checks ___. It keeps going until ___. Finally it shows ___, and that teaches ___."

Real vs Fake

In one sentence: our game turns a quick look at two products into a checking habit for real shopping.

  1. You start it (Messenger). Green flag, then PLAY. PLAY shouts start game.

  2. It gets ready (Box Keeper). The rules go into boxes: 12 seconds a round at level 1, 15 at level 2, 3 hearts, 10 points for each fake caught plus the seconds left. A list holds the 8 clues.

  3. It hides the fake (Decider). Pick random 1 to 2 tosses a coin: 1 puts the fake on the left, 2 on the right.

  4. You choose (Messenger). Clicking a product shouts try pick; the Stage sets Picked to 1 or 2.

  5. It judges (Decider). One IF: is Picked the same as Fake Side? Yes: points. No: lose a heart. The clue shows for 3 seconds either way.

  6. It repeats and gets harder (Loop & Timer Lead). A repeat until loop plays round after round until all rounds are done or the hearts run out. The timer measures how fast you were. From product 5, level 2 makes you read the label.

  7. What it finally does (Closer). No hearts left: the fakes win. Hearts left: you win the game star. The taskforce van arrives. The lesson: check the seal, NAFDAC number, expiry date and spelling before you buy.

Honest Scale

In one sentence: our game shows that cheating on the scale earns more today but loses customers tomorrow.

  1. You start it (Messenger). Green flag, then PLAY. The keys are events too: up arrow adds rice, down arrow removes rice, SPACE sells.

  2. It gets ready (Box Keeper). A kilo costs ₦1,500 and Mama T earns ₦200 on each honest kilo. Trust starts at 50: an honest sale adds 8, a cheat takes away 25.

  3. A customer waits (Loop & Timer Lead). Patience counts down every 0.1 seconds: a walker waits 16 seconds, a car only 10. Each scoop takes 0.3 seconds and moves the needle 1 kilo.

  4. It judges the sale (Decider). IF the needle equals what they wanted: honest, trust up. IF it is less: cheat, more profit today, trust down 25.

  5. It repeats (Loop & Timer Lead). A repeat until loop serves customers until all 8 are done or trust reaches 0.

  6. Customers decide (Decider). Below 30 trust, the next customer walks away to Iya Bisi.

  7. What it finally does (Closer). Night falls. Trust 60 or more: TRUSTED and the game star. Below 60: rich but not trusted. Below 1: the shop is closed. The lesson: honest work builds trust, and trust brings customers back.

Family Job Board

In one sentence: our game turns a busy day of chores into a race that rewards doing the urgent job first and keeping a steady streak.

  1. You start it (Messenger). Green flag, then PLAY. The day's clock starts at 0.

  2. It gets ready (Box Keeper). The day lasts 60 seconds. A normal chore gives 5 seconds and 10 points; an urgent one 3.5 seconds and 20 points. Every 3 in a row adds 5. A wrong spot costs 2. 120 is Gold, 70 is Silver.

  3. Mum picks a chore (Decider). Pick random 1 to 6 picks the chore (never the same twice in a row). A second roll from 1 to 100: 30 or less makes it urgent, in red.

  4. You click a spot (Messenger). The spot's IF asks: is Mum calling for MY chore? Yes: it shouts go do chore and Tayo walks there. No: minus 2 and the spot shakes.

  5. It waits and counts (Loop & Timer Lead). The game waits until the right spot, OR time up, OR the day ends. The loop repeats until the timer passes 57 seconds, and the sun moves to afternoon, then evening.

  6. Points add up (Box Keeper). 20 if urgent, 10 if normal, plus 5 for every third in a row. Time ran out? The streak goes back to 0.

  7. What it finally does (Closer). Daddy comes home and the IFs give the medal: Gold, Silver or Bronze. The lesson: do the urgent thing first, do it well, and keep a steady streak.

Anchor lesson: Real vs Fake (60 minutes)

Thirteen scenes, all three teams watching, the Real vs Fake team driving. Ask each question before you show the answer: guessing first is what makes it stick.

Before the clock starts: open Real vs Fake in the Scratch editor (not full screen), press Ctrl and + twice, start the screen recording, and check every child has their speech card and a pencil. Each Planner starts a timer.

  1. Honest start and the problem (3 min). Driver: none. Writing: Opener.

    • Say: "Our trainer prepared this starter game. Today we open it and see how every part works. Two products sit at Oga Sharp's stall. One is fake. Find it before time runs out."

    • Ask: "Has anyone at home bought something that turned out to be fake?"

  2. The green flag (4 min). Driver: Messenger clicks the green flag, then the Stage. Writing: Messenger.

    • Say: "When the green flag is clicked, every sprite with a when green flag clicked script runs at once. That is an event. They all hide, so the market is empty, like sweeping before visitors come."

  3. The rule book (6 min). Driver: Box Keeper opens set up the rules. Writing: Box Keeper.

    • Say: "Level 1 gives 12 seconds, level 2 gives 15. You start with 3 hearts. Each fake you catch is 10 points plus the seconds you had left. Each rule lives in its own box."

    • Ask: "How many wrong picks end the game?" (3)

  4. The clue list (5 min). Driver: Box Keeper opens fill the lists. Writing: Box Keeper.

    • Say: "Giveaways is a list: a notebook of 8 clues. Level 1 clues you SEE, like cloudy oil. Level 2 clues you READ, like an expiry date or no NAFDAC number."

    • Ask: "Is a wrong spelling a SEE clue or a READ clue?"

  5. The title broadcast (4 min). Driver: Messenger points at the title, PLAY, HOW TO PLAY and Oga Sharp. Writing: Messenger.

    • Say: "The Stage shouts show title. These sprites were listening, so they appear together."

    • Ask: "Who is the announcer and who are the listeners?"

  6. PLAY and the big loop (5 min). Driver: Loop & Timer Lead clicks PLAY, then opens the Stage's when I receive start game. Writing: Loop & Timer Lead.

    • Say: "The Stage shuffles the 8 products and starts a repeat until loop: keep playing rounds until all rounds are done, or the hearts run out."

    • Ask: "What are the two ways the game can end?"

  7. Hiding the fake (5 min). Driver: Decider opens get the next round ready and ticks Fake Side. Writing: Decider.

    • Say: "Pick random 1 to 2 is a coin toss. 1 means left, 2 means right. Even the person who made the game cannot know."

    • Do: play 3 rounds and let the room shout "left" or "right" before each one. Nobody can predict it.

  8. Your click (4 min). Driver: Messenger clicks a product; open when I receive try pick. Writing: Messenger.

    • Say: "Clicking shouts try pick. The Stage checks where the mouse is. Over the left product: Picked is 1. Over the right: Picked is 2."

  9. The judge (6 min). Driver: Decider opens play one round. Writing: Decider.

    • Say: "The Stage waits until you pick or the time runs out. Then one IF: is Picked equal to Fake Side? Yes: you caught it. No: you lose a heart. One question decides the whole round."

    • Ask: "Picked is 2 and Fake Side is 1. What happens?" (lose a heart)

  10. The stopwatch (5 min). Driver: Loop & Timer Lead points at the timer block and Time Left. Writing: Loop & Timer Lead.

    • Say: "Scratch has its own stopwatch called timer. The game remembers when the round started, so it knows how fast you were."

    • Ask: "You catch the fake with 8 seconds left. How many points?" (18)

  11. Level 2 and the ending (4 min). Driver: Decider plays to product 5, then opens end the game. Writing: Decider and Closer.

    • Say: "From product 5, an IF switches to LEVEL 2: now you must read the label. At the end, no hearts left means the fakes win; hearts left wins the game star. The taskforce van arrives either way."

  12. The live change (5 min). Driver: Logic Builder changes Base Points from 10 to 50, plays one round and picks fast. Writing: Logic Builder.

    • Ask first: "With 6 seconds left, what score will we get now?" (56)

    • Then change it back to 10. Always.

  13. Tell the chain (4 min). The Planner calls the speakers in the order of the process story in the section above. Each child says their one step out loud. The Closer finishes with what the game finally does and the lesson.

Your closing sentence: "Events start things, boxes remember, IFs decide, loops repeat, timers count down."

Art swap straight after (15 min, Real vs Fake team only). Import two premium costumes from the prompt pack: Oga Sharp smile and Heart full. Delete the old costume, rename the new one to exactly the old name, and play to check. Make the pictures yourself the night before, because image tools like ChatGPT require users to be 13 or older; the children choose, import and rename.

The 40-minute mission (the Planner reads each step aloud and keeps time):

  1. Find it (10 min). Each speaker finds their row in their own game and drags that script into view. Thumb up when found; the Planner ticks. Stuck after 2 minutes? Ask a teammate before the trainer.

  2. Teach it back and tell the chain (10 min). Each speaker explains their block in 40 seconds, then the team tells its process story in order, one step each.

  3. Live change (5 min). Predict out loud, the Logic Builder makes the change, everyone plays, then change it back.

  4. Art swap (15 min, Logic Builder leads). Honest Scale: Mama T idle and Trust Face green. Family Job Board: Tayo's walk costumes and Mum calm. Keep each costume name exactly the same and save as a new version (for example, "Honest Scale v3 art").

Your 10-minute check per team. Do not touch the mouse. Let any member answer by pointing:

  • "Show me where the game starts."

  • "Which box changes when ___?" (a sale is made, a chore is done)

  • "What question does your judging IF ask?"

  • "Tell me in 30 seconds what your game does, from PLAY to the ending."

Four answers without notes = the team earns its Stage Win. Any gap: send them back to that row or step, then check again before you leave.

What will make the biggest difference here

The Methodist children are small and curious, so they learn fastest by acting, guessing and holding things, not by watching a screen for long.

  1. Act the game before you open it (5 min). Real vs Fake: one child is Oga Sharp and hides a "fake" bottle in one hand behind their back (the coin toss), another picks, and the Planner counts down 12 seconds. Honest Scale: a cup of sand and a kitchen scale. Family Job Board: Mum calls a chore and Tayo runs to the right corner. Then open Scratch and say: "Now let's find where the computer does what we just did."

  2. Predict before every click. "What will happen?" before every run and every number change. A corrected wrong guess teaches more than an answer they were told.

  3. The process chain on a string. Write each step of the chain on its own card and give each card to the speaker who owns it. On stage, the children stand in chain order. If one forgets, the next child's card prompts them.

  4. The hand that does it explains it. Every speaker has driven the scene they will talk about. Judges can tell.

  5. Turn bugs into the best answer. Every Trouble Log line is a ready answer to "What was difficult?" One real bug told calmly beats a perfect demo.

  6. The thumbs-up words game (5 min, any time). Call out a word: broadcast, variable, IF, loop, timer, pick random. Only the speaker who owns it stands and gives a one-line example from their own game.

  7. Make the pictures the night before. Generate the premium art from the prompt pack at home and bring the PNG files on a USB stick, so the children only import and rename.

  8. A safe plan B. Two USB copies of every game, and the printed speech cards and chain cards in every child's pocket.

If the Planner should also speak on stage, give them one line on the Opener's card: "I kept our plan and our Trouble Log."
