Unity High School Showcase Teaching Guide

2026-10-19 · Olusoji Daniel Ayoola

The approach: teach Shrinking Naira deeply, then transfer

Teach Shrinking Naira in depth (70 minutes at most, which includes the signing pauses) with all three teams watching. Then the Middleman Maze and Empty Barn teams find the same parts in their own game in 40 minutes, led by their Planner, while you check. Do not teach all three in full: they share one skeleton, and two more full walk-throughs with signing would take over two hours.

Why Shrinking Naira is the anchor. It is the flagship game for the signed explanation, and it has the F freeze built in: press F and everything stops so the signer can catch up, then F again. It also teaches the idea that ties all three Unity games together: the game's memory lives in lists. Shrinking Naira keeps Prices and Status; Middleman Maze keeps the whole maze in the Maze list; The Empty Barn keeps Health and Since for the 9 stalls.

The skeleton all three games share:

  1. Green flag: every sprite hides; the Stage runs set up the rules.

  2. The Stage shouts show title; the title, buttons and characters appear.

  3. PLAY shouts start game; a loop runs the months, trips or the day.

  4. Lists remember the whole world of the game.

  5. Inside the loop: a timer counts, the player acts, IFs decide.

  6. The ending compares the results and teaches the lesson.

Sharing responsibilities

At every moment four jobs are running: one student drives (hands on the mouse), the Planner runs the clock and the checklist, the signing pair for that scene signs the key idea while the game is frozen, and every other speaker listens or watches for their own word and writes one line on the back of their speech card.

The Planner is always active. Pick the most organised child, not the best coder. The Planner:

  1. Holds a phone timer and calls "Switch!" (and signs it) when a scene's time is up.

  2. Holds up the next scene's key word, written big on a card, and names the next driver.

  3. Ticks the checklist and gives the thumbs-up when someone finds their block.

  4. Keeps the Trouble Log: one line each time something broke and how it was fixed (this becomes "the roadblocks we met").

  5. Saves each new version of the game with a new name and collects everyone's note lines at the end.

Signing pairs. Every speech card belongs to a pair: one partner signs, the other points at the screen. Both partners own the same part of the game, both write the line, and they swap who signs and who points during practice so either can cover on the day.

The driver is whoever the scene belongs to. Scene about lists: the Box Keeper drives. The hand that did it is the hand that explains it to the judges.

Four class rules that work for everyone:

  • Key word first. Before each scene, the Planner holds up the key word card and the trainer points at it, so nobody depends on hearing.

  • Freeze, then sign. Talk, press F, the pair signs, press F again. Never talk over the signing.

  • Thumbs up when you see or hear your word. You see at a glance who is following.

  • New scene, new driver. Nobody keeps the mouse two scenes in a row.

What each game does, start to finish

Every child must be able to tell their game's process as a chain: what the player does, what the game does with it, and what it finally shows. Each step names the speaker pair who owns it, so the chain is shared out and the team tells it together. The Closer copies their game's chain onto one card.

The 30-second pattern every child practises: "First you ___. The game remembers ___ in a list. It checks ___. It keeps going until ___. Finally it shows ___, and that teaches ___."

Shrinking Naira

In one sentence: our game shows that when prices rise, the same money buys less and less food.

  1. You start it (Messenger). Green flag, then PLAY. PLAY shouts start game.

  2. It gets ready (Box Keeper). Mum has ₦5,000 every month and it never grows. In month 1, rice ₦1,800, bread ₦1,200, eggs ₦900, tomatoes ₦600 and palm oil ₦500 add up to exactly ₦5,000. Each food has its own rise: rice goes up 20% a month.

  3. Lists remember the market (Box Keeper). Prices holds the 5 prices. Status holds 1 for "we still buy it" and 0 for "we gave it up".

  4. The months go by (Loop & Timer Lead). Month 1, then repeat 5 more: 6 months. Each month, prices climb in 12 small steps, 0.1 seconds apart; inside each step another loop raises all 5 foods.

  5. It adds up the basket (Decider). Start at 0, repeat 5 times: IF we still buy this food, add its price.

  6. Too expensive (Decider and Messenger). If the basket costs more than ₦5,000, a repeat until loop asks you to tap a food to put back, until it fits. That food's Status becomes 0.

  7. The freeze (Messenger). F shouts toggle freeze. One IF: not frozen? Freeze. Frozen? Unfreeze.

  8. What it finally does (Closer). The chart: in month 1, ₦5,000 bought all 5 foods; by month 6 the same foods cost ₦11,000. The money did not shrink; what it can buy shrank. That is inflation.

Middleman Maze

In one sentence: our game shows how much of the price reaches the farmer through middlemen, and how a co-op changes that.

  1. You start it (Messenger). Green flag, then PLAY. The Stage runs repeat 2: two trips.

  2. It gets ready (Box Keeper). The farmer is paid ₦10,000. The village buyer adds ₦5,000, the transporter ₦7,000, the market seller ₦8,000. The tomatoes rot after 45 seconds.

  3. A list is the map (Box Keeper). The Maze list has 7 rows of letters: # is a wall, a dot is road, 1, 2 and 3 are the middlemen, C is the co-op, M is the market.

  4. You drive (Messenger and Decider). Arrow keys or the arrow pad. The truck reads the letter of the next square: IF it is a wall, it wobbles and stays; ELSE it moves.

  5. The middlemen (Box Keeper). At each gate the truck shouts checkpoint. The price grows to ₦15,000, ₦22,000, then ₦30,000. The farmer's share stays ₦10,000.

  6. The trip clock (Loop & Timer Lead). Every 0.1 seconds, if no middleman is talking, Time Left goes down 0.1. The loop runs until you arrive OR time is up. Too late: everything becomes 0.

  7. The co-op (Decider). On trip 1, an IF treats C like a wall. On trip 2 it opens, and the farmer's share jumps to ₦20,000.

  8. What it finally does (Closer). The comparison: through middlemen the farmer got ₦10,000 of ₦30,000; through the co-op, ₦20,000 of ₦25,000. Working together cuts out the middle.

The Empty Barn

In one sentence: our game shows that catching sickness early, and preventing it, saves a farm.

  1. You start it (Messenger). Green flag, then PLAY. The barn opens.

  2. It gets ready (Box Keeper). The day lasts 60 seconds. A new animal may get sick every 4 seconds. Early signs last 4 seconds, very sick 5 more. An early catch is 20 points, a late one 10. Keep 6 animals to win. 3 vaccines.

  3. Lists remember the barn (Box Keeper). Health has one number per stall: 0 healthy, 1 early signs, 2 very sick, 4 lost. Since remembers when each stall last changed.

  4. Who gets sick? (Decider). Pick random 1 to 9 chooses a stall. If that animal is healthy, it gets early signs.

  5. Check every stall (Loop & Timer Lead). A loop of 9 asks the timer how long each sick animal has waited. Too long: it gets worse, or it is lost and the sickness spreads next door. The big loop runs until 60 seconds pass or no animals are left.

  6. You tap (Messenger). The animal's IF: early signs? +20. Very sick? +10.

  7. Vaccines (Decider). The vaccine button asks: is it daytime, and are there vaccines left? Yes: your next tap protects a healthy animal. No: buzz.

  8. What it finally does (Closer). Sunset. No animals left: an empty barn. 6 or more: a strong farm. Otherwise: weak. Act on early signs, and prevent before the problem starts.

Anchor lesson: Shrinking Naira (70 minutes)

Thirteen scenes, all three teams watching, the Shrinking Naira team driving and signing. "Sign" marks a pause: stop talking, press F if the game is running, let the scene's signing pair sign the key idea, then carry on. Ask each question before you show the answer.

Before the clock starts: open Shrinking Naira in the Scratch editor (not full screen), press Ctrl and + twice, start the screen recording, and check every child has their speech card and a pencil. Each Planner starts a timer and has the key word cards ready.

  1. Honest start and the problem (4 min). Driver: none. Writing: Opener pair. Key word: PRICES.

    • Say: "Our trainer prepared this starter game. Today we open it and see how every part works. Mum has ₦5,000 every month, while prices climb for 6 months."

    • Ask: "What costs more at home now than last year?" Sign.

  2. The green flag (4 min). Driver: Messenger clicks the green flag, then the Stage. Writing: Messenger pair. Key word: EVENT.

    • Say: "When the green flag is clicked, every sprite with a when green flag clicked script runs at once. They all hide, so the market is empty." Sign.

  3. The rule book (7 min). Driver: Box Keeper opens set up the rules. Writing: Box Keeper pair. Key word: BOX.

    • Say: "Mum has ₦5,000 every month, and it never grows. Each food has a start price and its own rise: rice goes up 20% every month."

    • Ask: "1,800 + 1,200 + 900 + 600 + 500. What is the total?" (₦5,000, exactly Mum's money) Sign.

  4. The title broadcast (4 min). Driver: Messenger points at the title, PLAY, HOW TO PLAY, Mum and Iya Oja. Writing: Messenger pair. Key word: BROADCAST.

    • Say: "The Stage shouts show title. These sprites were listening, so they come out together."

  5. PLAY and the months (6 min). Driver: Loop & Timer Lead clicks PLAY, then opens the Stage's when I receive start game. Writing: Loop & Timer pair. Key word: LOOP.

    • Say: "The Stage fills the price lists, starts month 1, then repeat 5: next month. Then Mum goes home and we see the chart."

    • Ask: "Month 1, then repeat 5. How many months altogether?" (6) Sign.

  6. The lists (6 min). Driver: Box Keeper shows the Prices and Status lists. Writing: Box Keeper pair. Key word: LIST.

    • Say: "Prices is a list of 5 numbers, one per food. Status is another list: 1 means we still buy it, 0 means we gave it up. Like a market list where you cross things out."

    • Ask: "Status says 0 for eggs. What does that mean for Mum?" Sign.

  7. Prices climb (8 min). Driver: Loop & Timer Lead goes to month 2 and opens prices climb. Writing: Loop & Timer pair. Key word: LOOP INSIDE A LOOP.

    • Say: "The outside loop runs 12 small steps, 0.1 seconds apart, so you can watch prices climb. Inside each step, another loop goes through all 5 foods. Every step first waits until Frozen is 0."

    • Ask: "Rice is ₦1,800 and goes up 20%. What is it next month?" (₦2,160) Press F. Sign. F again.

  8. The freeze IF (5 min). Driver: Messenger presses F, then opens when I receive toggle freeze. Writing: Decider pair. Key word: IF.

    • Say: "F shouts toggle freeze. The Stage asks: is the game on? Then: not frozen? Freeze. Already frozen? Unfreeze. One button, two jobs, decided by an IF." Sign.

  9. Adding up the basket (6 min). Driver: Decider opens add up the basket and ticks Basket Total ₦. Writing: Decider pair. Key word: ADD.

    • Say: "Start at 0. Repeat 5 times, once per food. IF we still buy it, add its price."

    • Ask: "In month 2 the basket costs ₦5,830. Mum has ₦5,000. What must happen?" Sign.

  10. Too expensive (6 min). Driver: Messenger taps a food to put back. Writing: Decider and Messenger pairs. Key word: CHOOSE.

    • Say: "A repeat until loop keeps going until the total fits. Each time, Mum asks YOU to choose a food to put back. Its Status becomes 0."

    • Ask: "Which food would you put back first, and why?" Sign.

  11. The ending (5 min). Driver: Closer points at the chart after month 6. Writing: Closer pair. Key word: INFLATION.

    • Say: "Month 1, ₦5,000 bought all 5 foods. By month 6 the same foods cost ₦11,000. Mum's money did not shrink. What it could BUY shrank. That is inflation." Sign.

  12. The live change (5 min). Driver: Logic Builder changes Money ₦ from 5000 to 8000 and plays to month 6. Writing: Logic Builder pair.

    • Ask first: "With ₦8,000, in which month does Mum first have to put food back?" (month 5; in month 4 the basket is ₦7,980 and just fits)

    • Then change it back to 5000. Always.

  13. Tell the chain (4 min). The Planner calls the pairs in the order of the process story above. Each pair says or signs their one step. The Closer pair finishes with what the game finally shows and the lesson.

Your closing sentence: "Events start things, lists remember, IFs decide, loops repeat, timers count down."

Art swap straight after (15 min, Shrinking Naira team only). Import two premium costumes from the prompt pack: Mum smile and Food f1 (rice). Delete the old costume, rename the new one to exactly the old name, and play to check. Make the pictures yourself the night before, because image tools like ChatGPT require users to be 13 or older; the children choose, import and rename.

Middleman Maze and The Empty Barn have no F freeze, so their pairs sign before and after the action: sign what is about to happen, play it, then sign what happened. Never sign while the clock is running.

The 40-minute mission (the Planner holds up each step's card and keeps time):

  1. Find it (10 min). Each pair finds their row in their own game and drags that script into view. Thumb up when found; the Planner ticks. Stuck after 2 minutes? Ask a teammate before the trainer.

  2. Teach it back and tell the chain (10 min). Each pair explains their block in 40 seconds (one speaks or signs, the other points), then the team tells its process story in order.

  3. Live change (5 min). Predict, the Logic Builder makes the change, everyone plays, then change it back.

  4. Art swap (15 min, Logic Builder leads). Middleman Maze: the Farmer happy and the Co-op Lady idle. The Empty Barn: the goat's healthy costumes (h1-1, h1-2) and the Farmhand stand. Keep each costume name exactly the same and save as a new version (for example, "Middleman Maze v3 art").

Your 10-minute check per team. Do not touch the mouse. Let any pair answer by pointing, speaking or signing:

  • "Show me where the game starts."

  • "Which list remembers the game, and what is in it?"

  • "What question does your main IF ask?"

  • "Tell me in 30 seconds what your game does, from PLAY to the ending."

Four answers without notes = the team earns its Stage Win. Any gap: send them back to that row or step, then check again before you leave.

What will make the biggest difference here

At Unity, the signing is the team's strength, not an extra. A judge who watches a pair explain a list with signs and pointing remembers it. These habits build on that.

  1. Act the game before you open it (5 min). Shrinking Naira: put 5 food cards on a table with price tags, give Mum ₦5,000 in paper notes, then raise the tags and let her choose what to put back. Middleman Maze: tape a maze on the floor and walk a "truck" through 3 children who each add to the price. The Empty Barn: 9 chairs as stalls. Then open Scratch: "Now let's find where the computer does what we just did."

  2. Predict before every click. "What will happen?" before every run and every number change, with an answer in signs or fingers ("show me the month").

  3. The process chain on a string. Write each step of the chain on its own card and give it to the pair who owns it. On stage the pairs stand in chain order, so the next card prompts anyone who forgets.

  4. The hand that does it explains it. Every pair has driven the scene they will explain.

  5. Turn bugs into the best answer. Every Trouble Log line is a ready answer to "What was difficult?" One real bug told calmly beats a perfect demo.

  6. The thumbs-up words game with signs (5 min, any time). Show a key word card: broadcast, list, IF, loop, timer. Only the pair who owns it stands; one signs, the other gives a one-line example from their own game.

  7. Make the pictures the night before. Generate the premium art from the prompt pack at home and bring the PNG files on a USB stick, so the children only import and rename.

  8. A safe plan B. Two USB copies of every game, printed speech cards and key word cards in every pocket, and the Shrinking Naira freeze button tested on the showcase computer.

If the Planner should also speak on stage, give them one line on the Opener's card: "I kept our plan and our Trouble Log."
