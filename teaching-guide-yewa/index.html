Yewa College Showcase Teaching Guide

2026-10-19 · Olusoji Daniel Ayoola

The approach: one anchor project, two transfer projects

In each track, teach one project in depth (60 minutes at most for Scratch, 45 for Web Dev and Gen AI). Then each other team applies the same logic to its own project in 40 minutes while you check. Do not teach all three in full. The three projects share one skeleton, so a second and third full walk-through costs about two more hours and loses the room.

Why one is enough: the three Yewa Scratch games are built from the same seven parts (green flag and hide, set up the rules, the show title broadcast, the start game recipe, IFs, a loop with a timer, the ending). The three web pages share the same numbered sections (the data, the variables, a loop, an IF, the events). The three Gen AI apps use the very same server.js file; only the page and the rule card (project.js) differ.

The Yewa anchors:

  • Scratch: Naira Wise. One tap moves ₦100 from one box to another, so every idea shows up within two minutes of play.

  • Web Dev: Harvest Calendar Naija. The smallest script: a list, a loop inside a loop and one IF, all in about 10 lines.

  • Gen AI: Inflation Explainer AI. The code does the maths and the AI only explains, which is the clearest picture of what code does and what AI does.

Record your screen while you teach each anchor (Xbox Game Bar: Windows + G). That recording replaces the old voice-over videos: on days you are not at the centre, the teams rewatch it. The same method works at Methodist and Unity with their own anchor game.

Who is working right now

At every moment in a lesson, exactly three jobs are running. One student drives (hands on the mouse). The Planner runs the clock and the checklist. Every other speaker listens for their own word and writes one line for their speech card.

The Planner is always active. Pick the most organised child in each team, not the best coder. The Planner:

  1. Holds a phone timer and calls "Switch!" when a scene's time is up.

  2. Reads the next step aloud and names the next driver.

  3. Ticks the checklist and gives the thumbs-up when a team member finds their block.

  4. Keeps the Trouble Log: one line for every time something broke and how it was fixed. This becomes the "roadblocks we met" part of the speech.

  5. At the end, collects every speaker's note line and reads them back once.

The Planner can also hold a speech card (Opener or Closer suits them best). In a 5-student Scratch team, some students hold two cards and listen for both words.

The driver is whoever the scene belongs to. When the scene is about boxes, the Box Keeper drives. The hand that does it is the mouth that will explain it on showcase day.

Three class rules:

  • Thumbs up when you hear your word. You see at a glance who is following, and it works silently for the Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing students too.

  • One line, own words, pencil. Not a copy of what the trainer said. A child who can write it in their own words can say it to a judge.

  • New scene, new driver. Nobody keeps the mouse for two scenes in a row.

Scratch anchor lesson: Naira Wise (60 minutes)

Twelve scenes, all three Scratch teams watching, the Naira Wise team driving. Each scene names the driver, who writes, what you say and one question for the room. Ask the question before you show the answer: guessing first is what makes it stick.

Before the clock starts: open Naira Wise in the Scratch editor (not full screen), press Ctrl and + twice so the blocks are readable, start the screen recording, and make sure every child has their printed speech card and a pencil. Each team's Planner starts a timer.

  1. Honest start and the problem (3 min). Driver: none. Writing: Opener.

    • Say: "Our trainer prepared this starter game. Today we open it up and see how every part works. Ada gets ₦1,000 a week for 3 weeks and splits every ₦100 note into SAVE, SPEND or GIVE."

    • Ask: "Who has ever spent all their money on the first day?"

  2. The green flag (5 min). Driver: Messenger clicks the green flag, then clicks the Stage. Writing: Messenger.

    • Say: "When the green flag is clicked, every sprite with a when green flag clicked script runs at the same time. That is an event. Almost all of them just say hide: we sweep the stage before visitors come."

    • Ask: "If we delete Ada's hide block, what will we see at the start?"

  3. The rule book (7 min). Driver: Box Keeper right-clicks set up the rules. Writing: Box Keeper.

    • Say: "These are Ada's rules, written into boxes. Pocket Money 1000. Note Value 100. Weeks 3. Week Time 12 seconds. The storybook costs 1500. Change a number here and the whole game changes."

    • Ask: "Which one box would you change to make the game harder?"

  4. The title broadcast (5 min). Driver: Messenger points at the title, PLAY, HOW TO PLAY and Ada. Writing: Messenger.

    • Say: "The Stage shouts one message: show title. The title, these 2 buttons and Ada are sprites listening for that broadcast. HOW TO PLAY explains nothing itself. It just shouts show help, and the help card listens."

    • Ask: "Who is the announcer and who are the listeners?"

  5. PLAY and the recipe cards (6 min). Driver: Loop & Timer Lead clicks PLAY, then opens the Stage's when I receive start game. Writing: Loop & Timer Lead and Messenger.

    • Say: "PLAY hides and shouts start game. The whole game is a few pink recipe cards: reset, then repeat Weeks, that is 3 times: start the week, split the money, end the week."

    • Ask: "If Weeks was 5, how many times would split the money run?"

  6. Tap the jars (6 min). Driver: Box Keeper taps SAVE 3 times with Pocket ₦ and Save ₦ ticked. Writing: Box Keeper.

    • Say: "Every tap moves ₦100 out of the Pocket box into the Save box. Pocket goes down, Save goes up."

    • Ask (before the taps): "Pocket is 1000. After 3 taps, what will it say?" (700)

  7. The IF in the jar (6 min). Driver: Decider clicks the Save Jar sprite and points at its IF. Writing: Decider.

    • Say: "Before the jar takes a note, it asks 3 questions, like a careful cashier. Is the game on? Is the clock not paused? Is there still ₦100 in the pocket? Only if all 3 are yes does it take the money."

    • Ask: "Pocket is 0 and I tap SAVE. What happens, and which question said no?"

  8. The week clock (6 min). Driver: Loop & Timer Lead opens split the money while the time bar shrinks. Writing: Loop & Timer Lead.

    • Say: "Every 0.1 seconds the loop takes 0.1 off Time Left, until time is up or the pocket is empty. A loop and a timer working together. A quarter of the way in, the hawker comes to tempt Ada."

    • Ask: "The week is 12 seconds. About when does the hawker come?" (about 3 seconds in)

  9. The week 2 surprise (3 min). Driver: Messenger plays on until the sandal breaks. Writing: Opener.

    • Say: "Ada's sandal breaks and the cobbler charges ₦300. This is why we save: the kolo is there for surprises."

    • Ask: "What surprise has cost your family money?"

  10. The ending (5 min). Driver: Decider opens choose the ending. Writing: Decider and Closer.

    • Say: "The Stage checks Ada's boxes in order, like a teacher marking a script. Did money leak away? Did she borrow? Did she spend 80% or more? The first yes picks the ending."

    • Ask: "Why does the order of the questions matter?"

  11. The live change (5 min). Driver: Logic Builder opens set up the rules and changes Note Value from 100 to 200, then plays and taps SAVE 5 times. Writing: Logic Builder.

    • Ask first: "With ₦200 notes, after 5 taps, how much is in Save?" (₦1,000, and the pocket is empty)

    • Then change it back to 100. Always.

  12. Say it back (3 min). Driver: none. The Planner calls each role; each child reads their one line aloud. The Closer ends: "Split your money the day you get it: some to SAVE, some to SPEND, some to GIVE."

Your closing sentence for every track: "Events start things, boxes remember, IFs decide, loops repeat, timers count down."

Art swap straight after (15 min, Naira Wise team only). Import two premium costumes from the prompt pack, Ada smile and Save Jar level 0, so everyone sees the rename rule: delete the old costume, rename the new one to exactly the old name, play to check. Generate the pictures on your own account and let the children direct the prompt and do the import; image tools like ChatGPT require users to be 13 or older.

The 40-minute mission (the Planner reads each step aloud and keeps time):

  1. Find it (10 min). Each speaker finds their row in their own game and drags that script into view. Thumb up when found; the Planner ticks. Stuck after 2 minutes? Ask a teammate before asking the trainer.

  2. Teach it back (10 min). Each speaker explains their block to the team in 40 seconds, using the same sentence they heard in the Naira Wise lesson but with their own game's words. Then they write their one line.

  3. Live change (5 min). The team predicts out loud, the Logic Builder makes the change, everyone plays, and the Logic Builder changes it back.

  4. Art swap (15 min). Swap in 2 to 4 premium costumes from the prompt pack: the main character first (the farmer, or the host), then one prop. Keep each costume name exactly the same. Save as a new version (for example, "Race Against Rot v3 art").

Your 10-minute check per team. Do not touch the mouse. Ask three things and let any member answer by pointing:

  • "Show me where the game starts."

  • "Which box changes when ___ happens?" (fill in from their game: a harvest is sold, an item is picked)

  • "What question does this IF ask?"

Three answers without reading notes = the team earns its Stage Win. Any gap: send them back to that row of the map, then check again before you leave.

Web Dev: teach Harvest Calendar Naija, transfer to the other two

Teach Harvest Calendar Naija in 45 minutes with all three web teams watching. Then Scam Shield and Apprentice Pathway Explorer find the same five ideas in their own script.js and add one creative change each. Line numbers below match the files in the Showcase Projects zip.

The anchor lesson (45 min). Open the folder in VS Code and index.html in the browser, side by side.

  1. Honest start (3 min). Driver: Messenger picks Jan, then clicks Cassava. Writing: Opener. Say: "Our trainer prepared the starter. We checked the data and made it ours."

  2. The house tour (5 min). Driver: Planner opens each file. Writing: everyone. Say: "index.html is the rooms of the house. style.css is the paint. script.js is the electricity: what happens when you press something." Ask: "If we delete style.css, does the page still work?" (yes, it just looks plain)

  3. The data (7 min). Driver: Box Keeper scrolls to const crops. Writing: Box Keeper. Say: "A list of crops, each one a box with the same labels: name, plant months, harvest months, note." Then add one crop together, save and refresh. It appears in the calendar. This is the moment they feel they built it.

  4. The variable (5 min). Driver: Box Keeper points at line 28. Writing: Box Keeper. Say: "currentMonth remembers today's month. JavaScript counts January as 0, so we add 1." Ask: "What would break if we forgot the + 1?"

  5. The loop inside a loop (7 min). Driver: Loop & Timer Lead points at lines 41 and 43. Writing: Loop & Timer Lead. Say: "For every crop, check all 12 months. Like a class teacher calling every name, then ticking 12 boxes beside each name." Ask: "6 crops times 12 months: how many boxes does the loop fill?" (72)

  6. The IF (7 min). Driver: Decider points at lines 44 to 49. Writing: Decider. Say: "Planting and harvesting in the same month? Colour it both. Only planting? Plant. Only harvesting? Harvest."

  7. The events (5 min). Driver: Messenger clicks a crop name, then changes the month picker. Writing: Messenger. Say: "addEventListener means: when this is clicked, run these lines. The page waits and listens, like a sprite with when this sprite clicked."

  8. The live change (3 min). Driver: Logic Builder changes line 28 to let currentMonth = 12; saves and refreshes, then changes it back. Writing: Logic Builder.

  9. Say it back (3 min). The Planner calls each role; each child reads their line.

Then explain what you changed in 3 simple sentences for a 12-year-old." The rule: no change stays in the project unless someone on the team can explain it in one sentence. The Planner asks "Who can explain this?" before saving.

Gen AI: teach Inflation Explainer AI, transfer to the other two

Teach Inflation Explainer AI in 45 minutes with all three Gen AI teams watching, in demo mode (no key needed). Then Community Voice AI and Naija Career Compass AI find the same parts in their own app. All three apps share the identical server.js, so once one team understands it, all three do.

The anchor lesson (45 min). Start the app with node server.js and open the page. The demo banner shows: say that out loud, because it is honest.

  1. Honest start (3 min). Driver: Messenger fills in bread, 300, 2020, 350, 2024 and presses Explain it. Writing: Opener. Say: "Our trainer prepared the starter. We wrote the rules the AI must follow."

  2. The restaurant tour (7 min). Driver: Planner opens each part. Writing: Safety Officer. Say: "The page is the menu and the waiter. project.js is the chef's rule card. server.js is the kitchen door. The key that pays for the cooking stays in a locked drawer called .env, and only the adult holds it." Ask: "Why must a customer never walk into the kitchen with the key?"

  3. Our own maths (7 min). Driver: Box Keeper points at lines 20 to 27 in public/script.js. Writing: Box Keeper. Say: "The page reads 5 boxes from the form. Then line 27 works out the percentage ourselves. We never trust the AI with maths." Ask (before showing): "₦300 to ₦350: what percent is that?" (16.7%)

  4. The check before the AI (7 min). Driver: Decider opens validate in project.js, then types a 1-letter item and presses Explain it. Writing: Decider. Say: "Bad orders are sent back before we spend any money on the AI. Item too short? Price not a positive number? Stop." Let the room try to break it for 2 minutes. They love this.

  5. The chef's rule card (8 min). Driver: Safety Officer reads instructions in project.js aloud. Writing: Safety Officer. Say: "These sentences are our prompt. They say who the reader is (ages 9 to 15), what to write (3 short parts), and what never to do (no investing advice, no made-up facts)." Ask: "Which rule protects the user most?" In demo mode the answer is a sample, so changes to the rules only show when you switch on the live AI.

  6. The loop and the speed limit (5 min). Driver: Loop & Timer Lead points at line 21 in project.js, then tooMany in server.js. Writing: Loop & Timer Lead. Say: "Line 21 checks all 4 numbers, one after the other. tooMany counts questions: more than 20 in one minute from one computer, and it says wait."

  7. The live change (5 min). Driver: Logic Builder changes "* 1000) / 10;" to "* 100);" on line 27, saves, refreshes and runs bread again. Writing: Logic Builder. Ask first: "What will 16.7% become?" (17%) Then change it back.

  8. Say it back (3 min). The Planner calls each role; each child reads their line.

What will make the biggest difference in five days

The children grow fastest when they guess, do and explain, not when they watch. These eight habits do that.

  1. Predict before every click. Ask "What will happen?" before every run and every number change. A wrong guess that gets corrected teaches more than a right answer they were told.

  2. The hand that does it explains it. The driver rule above means every speaker has touched the block they will talk about. Judges can tell the difference.

  3. Make the pictures the night before. Generate the premium art from the prompt pack at home and bring the PNG files on a USB stick. On the day, the children only import and rename, which they can do without you.

  4. Turn bugs into the best part of the speech. Every Trouble Log line is a ready answer to "What was difficult?" One real bug, told calmly, impresses a judge more than a perfect demo.

  5. The thumbs-up words game (5 min, any time). Call out a word: broadcast, variable, IF, loop, timer, validate. Only the speaker who owns it stands and gives a one-line example from their own project. It warms them up and checks understanding in one go, and it works for the Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing students with signed words.

  6. Same-skeleton quiz. Show one block from another team's game and ask, "What is this in YOUR game?" When a child can answer that, the transfer has worked.

  7. One phone video per speaker. Watch it once, name one thing done well and one fix. Not more: confidence grows from small fixes.

  8. A safe plan B for the day. Two USB copies of every project, the Gen AI apps tested in demo mode in case the internet fails, and printed cards in every pocket.

Open question: the speech cards have no Planner card. If your Planner should also speak, the best fit is a short line in the Opener's card about how the team worked ("I kept our plan and our Trouble Log").
